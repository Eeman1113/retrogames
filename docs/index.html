Boss fight · the paradox

“Later games are better” is false, and the data says so twice

Correlation between a game's release lag — years after its platform launched — and its canon score. Hover a bar for what it means.

Pool all 205 picks and the correlation is strong and positive, which looks like proof that developers get better with practice. Centre both columns on each system's own mean and it collapses to nothing. The pooled number was never about games: platforms whose canon arrives late are platforms that lasted, and long-lived platforms have stronger canons. Same data, opposite conclusion — a textbook Simpson's paradox, and the reason this notebook exists.

How the score works, and what it can't do

canon_score is a curated 0–100 index of how strongly a title sits in the retro canon. It is an editorial construct, not a scraped review aggregate, and the notebook treats it as the suspect variable throughout: it re-runs the headline result 2,000 times with ±5 points of simulated curator disagreement (the sign holds in 100% of draws) and collapses the scale to rank-only as a second check.

Only 22% of picks have a published sales figure, and the gap is structured — no arcade-only title has one, and almost no Japan-only release does. That is missing-not-at-random data, so the notebook tests the missingness instead of dropping it. Two hardware figures are estimates rather than measurements: Nintendo reports NES+Famicom as one number, and Game Boy+Game Boy Color as another.

These are the top five per system, so nothing here describes the average game on a platform — only its ceiling.
